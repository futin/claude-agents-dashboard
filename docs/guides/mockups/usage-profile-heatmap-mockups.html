Usage profile inspector

Three ways to show the learned duty-cycle profile — the 168 hour-of-week weights the weekly forecast walks over. Pick one; the differences are about which screen you read it on and how directly it explains the projected number.

Synthetic data. Each cell is one hour of the week — Monday 09:00 is a different cell from Tuesday 09:00, and nothing is averaged across days. What accumulates across weeks is the evidence: a cell can gather at most 60 minutes per week, so "5 weeks of evidence" means five separate Mondays. Weight = the share of that hour you are typically active. Hatched = not enough evidence yet, which is a different thing from zero.

Variant A — days as rows

24 columns across. Reads like a calendar week and the daily rhythm pops out immediately. On a phone each cell lands near 9 px wide.

Variant B — hours as rows

Seven columns, so it stays legible held in one hand — the axis that needs 24 slots gets the direction a phone actually has. Comparing two days means reading down two columns instead of across two rows.

Variant C — Variant B plus the walk that produced the number

The grid alone says when you work. It still does not say why the forecast said Monday. This adds the forward walk underneath: one bar per hour from now to the weekly reset, height = that hour's contribution, and the mark where the running total crosses 100%.

now · Fri 18:00 · 60% used

Flat grey stubs are hours the profile expects to be idle — they contribute nothing, which is the whole point of the feature. The gap across the weekend is what stops the projection landing on Saturday.

Table view

Present because two of the five ramp steps fall below 3:1 contrast against the card surface. A low-contrast cell is fine as an encoding — barely-there means barely-active — but the numbers have to be reachable some other way.

Considered and dropped

A second colour for confidence. The obvious move is to hue-code evidence — say amber for thin cells over the cyan ramp. It fails: two hues on one cell reads as a rainbow ramp, and confidence is not a magnitude on the same scale as weight. An untrusted cell has no value, so it gets texture and a dashed edge instead. Absence should look like absence.

Line chart of weight by hour. Cheaper to build, but it flattens the week into one axis, so Tuesday and Saturday overlay each other and the weekday/weekend split — the single most useful thing in the data — disappears.
